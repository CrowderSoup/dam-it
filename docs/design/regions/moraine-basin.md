# Moraine Basin — Visual Reference

Status: **Approved direction**

Moraine Basin is a steep glacially shaped headwater landscape. Conifers,
layered rock, burned snags, deadfall, lingering snow, fast water, and resilient
new growth show both the older wildfire and the beginning of recovery.

## Emotional read and composition

Moraine Basin is exposed, steep, and visibly wounded, but never apocalyptic.
Diagonal runoff paths cut through terraces of mixed stone and lingering snow.
Clusters of surviving conifers and aspen, low shoots, and small wet refuges
prevent charcoal from owning the whole image. The basin's glacial bowl lets the
player read how several tributaries connect before building.

Key landmarks are Bramble's repeatedly damaged shelter, the eventual
high-ground den, a moraine overlook, three runoff paths, an exposed bank, a
snowfield feeding the spring surge, and the downstream outlet toward Aspen
Meadow.

## Before and after

- **Before:** Runoff races through a few incised paths, loose sediment clouds
  the water, deadfall points downhill, and Bramble's low shelter bears repeated
  damage.
- **Transition:** Small porous dams visibly slow—not stop—flow; braces catch
  selected logs and sediment; reopened side channels wet several safe pockets.
- **After:** The fire remains legible, but water follows a complex connected
  network, small refuges hold clear edges, the high-ground den is active, and
  shoots appear where moisture persists.

During the spring surge, preserve both danger and readability: snow edges
recede, water darkens and quickens, caught debris strains rather than vanishes,
and resident movement shows the distributed system working.

## Motion and sound cues

Use stronger wind, snow drip, distant runoff, creaking snags, loose pebbles, and
occasional debris knocks before restoration. Add separated side-channel sounds,
quieter protected pockets, resident work, and small bird or insect returns
without making the basin serene too early.

## Avoid

- An all-black disaster landscape.
- Flames, active fire, or imagery that shifts the game into catastrophe horror.
- Instant mature forest regrowth.
- A giant final wall that appears to eliminate flood risk.
- Clearing every snag; retain safe deadwood as part of the recovering habitat.
