# Aspen Meadow — Visual Reference

Status: **Approved direction**

Aspen Meadow is an open mid-elevation wet meadow where spring is still
arriving. Pale trunks, branching channels, sedge hummocks, lingering snow, and
small early wildflowers create long, readable sightlines.

## Emotional read and composition

Aspen Meadow should feel spacious and contested rather than empty. White trunks
form loose vertical rooms around a broad horizontal meadow. Lingering snow and
dry hummocks show that spring has not reached every patch equally. From one
overlook, the player can understand both possible water configurations before
committing.

Key landmarks are Marnie's depleted pool, Clover's vulnerable low warren, the
water-control junction, Saffron's isolated flower margin, the protected
high-ground warren, and paired overlooks that reframe the current configuration.

## Configuration contrast

### Deep pool

Use one broad reflective water shape, a strong nesting-island silhouette,
submerged former path markers, larger pond plants, and concentrated duck
motion. Keep a smaller but healthy flower margin for Saffron.

### Braided meadow

Use several narrow glinting channels, damp color spreading between sedge
hummocks, multiple crossings, animated flower clusters, and pollinator movement
across the full view. Keep a modest open side pool for Marnie.

Switching configuration must recompose traversal, resident routines, sound,
and the payoff overlook—not merely swap props.

## Motion and sound cues

Aspen leaves should quake in light wind; channels add spatially separated water
sounds. The deep pool emphasizes waterfowl, lapping water, and broad reflections.
The braided meadow emphasizes insects, flowers moving in waves, shallow flow,
and calls distributed across the space.

## Avoid

- Presenting either configuration as damaged or morally inferior.
- Turning the braided state into a uniform flower carpet.
- Turning the deep-pool state into a featureless lake.
- Hiding the water-choice consequences outside the player's view.
