# Willowbend — Visual Reference

Status: **Approved direction**

Willowbend is a sheltered lower foothill creek near the mouth of a fictional
Wasatch-inspired canyon. Early spring reaches it first. Curving banks, willow
roots, emerging cottonwoods, scrub-oak slopes, and warm stone enclose the
protagonist's first home.

## Emotional read and composition

Willowbend begins intimate but depleted: a remembered home reduced to a thin
blue line through exposed mud. Curving banks, enclosing scrub-oak slopes, and
overlapping willow stems make the region feel sheltered. The restored pond and
lodge become a warm center rather than expanding the map outward.

Key landmarks are the last shallow pool, remnants of Grandma Hazel's era of
dams, the lodge site, Eddy's downstream gravel, a cottonwood at the route to
Aspen Meadow, and a high overlook used for the pond-transformation shot.

## Before and after

- **Before:** Exposed banks dominate, willow buds remain tight, the last pool
  barely shelters Moss, and Eddy is confined below the broken dam remnants.
- **Transition:** Water backs into irregular low ground, darkens soil, carries
  small sticks into side pockets, and tests Eddy's flow route before the build
  is accepted.
- **After:** A layered pond reconnects wet margins; buds open, insects and a
  frog chorus return, Eddy moves among flow types, and warm lodge light reflects
  in the water.

## Motion and sound cues

Start with exposed trickle, light canyon wind, and isolated frog calls. Add
willow-tip movement, cottonwood flutter, insects, overlapping calls, deeper
water animation, and gentle lodge activity as the habitat returns.

## Avoid

- A uniformly saturated green lawn.
- A rectangular pond or perfectly straight dam.
- Mature vegetation appearing instantly.
- Making Willowbend so lush at the opening that restoration is hard to read.
