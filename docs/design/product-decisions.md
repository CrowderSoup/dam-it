## 1. North-star pitch and playtime

**Decision:** Use this north-star pitch and retain the **2–4 hour** main-story
target:

> Play as a young beaver restoring a chain of neglected wetlands. Build dams,
> reshape waterways, and bring habitats and a community back to life—then help
> the watershed weather one final storm.

**Intended takeaway:** Six months after playing, a player should remember that
beavers are ecosystem engineers. The game should teach this through enjoyable
cause and effect—players build, water changes, habitats appear, and animals
respond—not through lessons pasted onto the play.

**Why:** The pitch names the player fantasy, visible transformation, community
payoff, and climax without implying a large sandbox simulation. The length
reinforces the roadmap's “small, polished, and finishable” pillar.

**Guardrail:** The 2–4 hours covers the main story for a first-time player. It
does not include post-credits free play or completing every optional habitat
improvement.

**Accuracy rule:** Ecological relationships and outcomes should be broadly
authentic, while time, distance, construction effort, and animal behavior may
be compressed for fun.

**Status:** Approved 2026-09-14

## 2. Why the protagonist comes to the creek

**Decision:** The protagonist has recently left their family territory to find
a place of their own. They follow an old waterway to Willowbend, expecting to
prove they can build a lodge independently. The diminished creek makes that
private goal impossible: restoring enough water for a home also restores
habitats other animals depend on. By the ending, their idea of “my home” has
become “our watershed.”

They begin as a **capable apprentice**. Their family taught them the basics of
dam-building, and they understand that a dam creates a pond, but this is their
first independent home. They do not yet appreciate how extensively their
choices can reshape habitats upstream and downstream.

**Why:** This gives the player an immediate, concrete want (water and a lodge),
explains why they are willing to do the work, and creates an internal arc that
matches the construction loop. It avoids a chosen-one premise and keeps the
stakes personal and cozy.

**Opening proof point:** Within the first few minutes, the player should see the
dry lodge site, attempt or discuss settling there, and learn that the creek no
longer holds enough water.

**Status:** Approved 2026-09-14

## 3. Final story theme

**Decision:**

> **A lasting home is built by caring for the whole living community around
> it.**

**Why:** The sentence connects the protagonist's personal goal, habitat
restoration, construction decisions, the returning cast, the raccoon's
displacement, and the final storm. “Built” works literally and emotionally;
“living community” includes both the named residents and the wider ecosystem.
It also gives writers a test: character and building beats should show the
tension between making a place for yourself and caring for the wider watershed.

**Tone boundary:** The story can acknowledge neglect, displacement, and storm
damage without blaming the player, preaching at them, or turning ecological
recovery into a grim catastrophe narrative.

**Status:** Approved 2026-09-14

## 4. Region names and visual identities

**Decision:** Set the game in a fictional watershed inspired by Utah's Wasatch
Mountains. Its ecology, hydrology, geology, and seasons should feel authentic
to the Wasatch Front without claiming to reproduce one real creek. The journey
moves upstream and upward over several in-world spring weeks, compressed into
short act-transition montages.

### Willowbend

- **Role:** A sheltered lower foothill creek near the canyon mouth and the
  protagonist's first home. Early spring has reached it first.
- **Shape language:** Curving close banks, willow roots, stepping stones,
  emerging cottonwoods, scrub-oak slopes, and a pond that becomes the region's
  cozy center.
- **Palette:** Warm quartzite gray, willow gold-green, cool creek blue, damp
  earth, and lodge amber. Little or no snow remains.
- **Transformation:** A thin, incised channel reconnects with wet ground and
  becomes a layered pond edged by opening willow buds, water plants, insects,
  fish, and occupied homes.

### Aspen Meadow

- **Role:** An open mid-elevation wet meadow where water must be shared between
  habitats as spring climbs the mountain.
- **Shape language:** Broad horizontal spaces, pale aspen trunks, branching
  channels, sedge hummocks, lingering snow patches, and long sightlines.
- **Palette:** Fresh yellow-green, white aspen, reed gold, snow-shadow blue, and
  small bursts of glacier-lily and yellow-bell color.
- **Transformation:** Dry, uniform grass becomes a patchwork of pools, damp
  meadow, spring flowers, and safe paths rather than one completely flooded
  area.

### Moraine Basin

- **Role:** Steep headwaters in a glacially shaped basin where an older wildfire
  left exposed slopes, displaced residents, deadfall, and unstable runoff.
  Residents discuss the fire and its continuing effects.
- **Shape language:** Diagonal channels, moraine terraces, conifer silhouettes,
  aspen stands, layered rock, burned snags, lingering snow, and fast narrow
  water.
- **Palette:** Slate and buff stone, snow white, pine green, charcoal, cold
  runoff blue, and small accents of resilient new growth.
- **Transformation:** The player cannot erase the burn. They can reconnect the
  stream with small floodplain areas, catch some sediment and woody debris,
  stabilize vulnerable channels, and create wet refuges where recovery is
  visibly beginning.

### Seasonal climax

A warm spring storm arrives near peak runoff. Rain and rising temperatures
accelerate snowmelt from Moraine Basin, testing whether the connected wetlands
can slow, spread, and hold the surge. The restored watershed improves the
outcome but is never presented as eliminating all flood risk.

**Why:** The three identities form a readable elevation and seasonal gradient,
from intimate thawed creek to open wet meadow to rugged snowy headwaters. The
names describe beloved features rather than defining Moraine Basin by its fire.
Several compressed weeks allow buds, flowers, resident homes, and runoff to
change visibly without suggesting that a mature wetland appears in minutes.

**Research anchors:** [Wasatch geology and glacial landforms](https://geology.utah.gov/popular/utah-landforms/virtual-tour-central-wasatch-front-canyons/),
[Wasatch snowmelt hydrology](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/estimation-baseflow-and-flooding-characteristics-east-canyon-creek-summit-and-morgan),
[Utah beaver-dam habitat restoration](https://extension.usu.edu/swaner/on-the-preserve/conservation-projects/stream-restoration),
and [post-fire snowmelt response](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/69718).

**Status:** Approved 2026-09-14

## 5. Player name and identity

**Decision:** Use a **soft-authored protagonist** with a fixed name, basic
history, and the capable-apprentice motivation. Select the final name alongside
the resident names during the cast pass.

There is no appearance creator or selectable backstory. The protagonist's
gender is unspecified; residents normally use their name or “you.” Routine
dialogue imposes little personality, while occasional choices let the player
respond earnestly, practically, or playfully. These choices express tone and
receive acknowledgement but do not branch the main plot.

**Why:** A compact authored story benefits from residents addressing the
protagonist naturally and referring to shared events without text-entry UI or
large dialogue branches. Restrained characterization and response-tone choices
still leave room for players to project themselves into the role.

**Guardrail:** Keep the characterization light enough that players can inhabit
the role. Define what the protagonist wants and how that changes, but avoid a
large off-screen biography.

**Status:** Approved 2026-09-14

## 6. Construction-choice permanence

**Decision:** Functional construction choices are **reversible at their
structure for a modest material cost after a clear preview**. Residents
remember and may comment on the player's first completed choice, while the
world reflects the current configuration. Revising a choice never locks story
progress, removes a resident, or requires restarting a save.

Use three clear categories:

- Fixed story builds, such as the first dam, are permanent milestones.
- Functional choices, such as routing water between two habitats, can be
  rebuilt later for a modest cost.
- Cosmetic improvements can be changed freely.

**Why:** This preserves experimentation and the cozy no-failure promise while
allowing choices to have authored acknowledgement. The rebuild cost keeps the
decision tangible. Monitoring an ecological intervention and adapting it after
seeing the outcome also reinforces responsible stewardship rather than
treating revision as failure.

**Implementation constraint:** Every reversible choice must show its affected
areas before confirmation and provide the revision point in the world—not only
in a menu.

**Status:** Approved 2026-09-14

## 7. Release platforms and storefront scope

**Decision:** Release 1.0 as a **free browser game**. GitHub Pages remains the
initial host. The supported experience is a desktop browser with keyboard or
gamepad; touch controls, mobile layouts, downloadable desktop builds,
storefronts, and monetization are outside the baseline.

The long-term direction is a Web game library where signed-in players can pick
games, resume progress across devices, and see cross-game status such as
started/completed state, playtime, and achievements. That library, user
accounts, cloud storage, and achievements are **not** part of this game's 1.0.

To avoid blocking the library later:

- Give the game a stable machine-readable ID and keep its save schema versioned.
- Keep serialization of game state separate from the storage backend, so local
  browser storage can later be replaced or supplemented by a library service.
- Preserve two future browser interfaces: one to import/export complete save
  payloads and one to report game events such as started, completed, playtime,
  and achievements.
- Keep the standalone game fully playable when no library service is present.
- Before changing the game's public origin or host, provide a save export/import
  or migration path; browser-local saves do not automatically follow a move.
- Avoid coupling core game logic to a particular portal, account provider, or
  platform SDK.

**Why:** A free Web release builds directly on the existing automated export
and matches the goal of finishing a first game. The lightweight integration
boundaries protect the future library idea without making this project build
the library prematurely.

**Technical reference:** Godot Web exports can be embedded in a containing page
and can communicate with browser JavaScript through `JavaScriptBridge`.

**Status:** Approved 2026-09-14
