# Dialogue Style

Status: **Approved**  
Milestone: **0 — Vision lock**

## Established constraints

- The game is all-ages, centered on independent readers around age 8 and up.
  Clear language must not make the writing feel like children's educational
  software or talk down to older cozy-game players.
- The protagonist is soft-authored and leaves room for player projection.
- Occasional response choices express an earnest, practical, or playful tone
  without branching the main plot.
- The protagonist's gender is unspecified; residents normally use their name
  or “you.”
- Ecological information should normally be demonstrated through play and
  environmental change rather than delivered as a lecture.

## Length and pacing

- Use one or two sentences per panel and normally no more than 25–30 words.
- Routine conversations last 2–4 panels. Major act scenes may reach 6–8 panels
  when the player advances each one.
- Grandma Hazel's letters stay under approximately 120 words.
- Ordinary dialogue never auto-advances.

## Language and ecological concepts

- Aim for a comfortable grade 3 reading level without flattening character
  voice or emotional nuance. Place names and one visible nature word may read
  above that score; explain their meaning with short, concrete sentences.
- Introduce at most one unfamiliar ecological term at a time, in a scene where
  the player can see or interact with its meaning.
- Prefer a resident's concrete observation over a definition. Eddy might say
  that silt is covering the gravel before anyone names sediment transport.
- Do not restate an ecological effect in dialogue when the animation, sound,
  resident behavior, and objective already make it clear.

## Humor and tone

- Build humor from personality, timing, mismatched perspectives, and occasional
  gentle wordplay.
- Avoid memes, topical references, constant animal puns, or jokes that undercut
  sincere relationship and recovery beats.
- No resident is mocked for a habitat need, fear, mistake, or reading level.

## Interruptions and ambient dialogue

- Most conversations are initiated by the player.
- Mandatory dialogue is limited to region arrivals, major transformations, and
  immediate hazards. It does not repeatedly interrupt gathering or travel.
- Ambient lines contain one short sentence, do not stop movement, respond to a
  meaningful world state, and avoid rapid repetition.

## Player responses

- Offer response choices only at meaningful relationship beats.
- Use no more than three concise tonal options: earnest, practical, or playful.
- Choices receive a character-specific acknowledgement but do not hide a wrong
  answer or branch the main story.

## Readability test

An independent 8-year-old reader should understand a line on the first read,
while an adult should not feel that the line was written only for a child.
