## Personality and voice

### Moss

Dry-witted, observant, and guarded. Moss notices environmental changes before
other residents do and uses understatement instead of open enthusiasm. The
guardedness softens visibly as Reed proves they intend to remain.

### Marnie

Energetic, decisive, and too quick to offer a solution. Marnie's confidence is
useful in a crisis, but Aspen Meadow teaches her that leadership means listening
and coordinating rather than always having the first answer.

### Eddy

Calm, precise, and sensitive to subtle differences in depth, current, and
sediment. Eddy provides ecological detail through direct observations of lived
conditions rather than lectures.

### Clover

Practical, prepared, and resistant to uncontrolled change—not cowardly. Clover
knows Aspen Meadow's ground and routes better than anyone and becomes the cast's
best planner of safe paths and refuges.

### Saffron

Curious, direct, and accustomed to residents treating seasonal visitors as
less invested in a place. Saffron challenges the assumption that temporary
habitat or migration routes matter less than permanent homes.

### Bramble

Resourceful, proud, defensive, and quietly funny. Bramble can solve immediate
physical problems but struggles to expose a need or ask for help. Accountability
does not erase the wit or independence.

## Relationship friction

- Moss judges Bramble too quickly after the first theft.
- Marnie talks over Clover's local knowledge when proposing the first Aspen
  Meadow solution.
- Saffron challenges residents who treat seasonal habitat as less important.
- None of these disagreements make a resident cruel or turn one habitat need
  into the morally correct choice.

## Supporting character — Grandma

- **Name:** Hazel
- **Species:** Beaver
- **Role:** The protagonist's living grandmother, born at Willowbend
- **Presence:** Brief written replies between acts; she does not appear as a
  resident or assign local objectives
- **Story function:** Connects the protagonist to Willowbend, marks the passage
  of several spring weeks, and reflects their changing idea of home
- **Voice boundary:** Warm and experienced, offering perspective rather than
  knowing the correct answer to each region's problem
